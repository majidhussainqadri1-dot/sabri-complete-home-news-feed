## 1. Editorial mission

The Newsroom exists to publish accurate, sourced, clearly labelled, privacy-respecting, and publicly accountable information related to Classical Homeopathy, medical and public-health education, research, professional development, institutional activity, and the Sabri Homeopathy platform.

Speed does not override accuracy. Popularity does not override evidence. Institutional authority does not override correction duties. Editorial independence does not override lawful privacy and safety requirements.

## 2. Separation of content classes

### 2.1 Straight News

Straight News reports verified events, statements, publications, regulatory actions, research, and institutional developments. It must distinguish fact from interpretation.

### 2.2 Analysis

Analysis interprets verified facts and must be labelled `Analysis`. It may present reasoned conclusions but must identify assumptions, uncertainty, and competing interpretations.

### 2.3 Editorial and Opinion

Editorial or Opinion content represents a declared institutional or named-author viewpoint. It must not be styled as neutral straight News.

### 2.4 Press Release and Supplied Content

A press release may be summarized or published only with a visible `Press Release` or equivalent supplied-content label unless the Newsroom independently verifies and substantially reports the matter.

### 2.5 Sponsored and Partner Content

Sponsored or partner material must:

- be visibly labelled before the headline or at the top of the article;
- identify the sponsor or partner;
- remain subject to privacy, medical-safety, evidence, copyright, and correction rules;
- never purchase editorial approval of an unsupported medical claim;
- remain visually distinguishable from independent reporting.

## 3. Source hierarchy

Sources are evaluated by relevance, proximity, competence, independence, transparency, and verifiability.

Preferred hierarchy:

1. Original research paper, official dataset, regulation, judgment, institutional record, or direct verified statement.
2. Authoritative secondary synthesis from a recognized institution or scholarly source.
3. Established professional or news publication with transparent reporting standards.
4. Supporting contextual source.
5. Press release or supplied material.
6. Unverified claim requiring further evidence.

A source is not reliable merely because it is official, popular, highly ranked, or aligned with the platform’s position. Commercial interest, methodology, publication status, corrections, and conflicts must be considered.

## 4. Research and evidence reporting

Research News must identify, where material:

- study design;
- population or sample;
- intervention/exposure and comparator;
- primary outcome;
- publication or preprint status;
- relevant effect size and uncertainty;
- limitations;
- funding and conflicts;
- whether findings are laboratory, animal, observational, survey, trial, review, or regulatory evidence;
- whether the result is replicated or preliminary.

The article must not:

- rewrite correlation as causation;
- treat a preprint as peer-reviewed evidence;
- generalize beyond the studied population without qualification;
- infer clinical effectiveness from laboratory or animal evidence alone;
- hide negative, null, or conflicting findings that materially change interpretation;
- convert statistical significance into guaranteed clinical importance;
- present an abstract, press release, or commercial summary as though it were the full study.

## 5. Homeopathy reporting standard

Reporting about Homeopathy must separate:

- historical doctrine;
- professional or institutional opinion;
- practitioner experience;
- case report;
- observational evidence;
- controlled clinical evidence;
- laboratory research;
- public-health or regulatory claims.

The Newsroom may report supportive, critical, mixed, or uncertain evidence, but the article must accurately describe source quality and limitations. Institutional belief or clinical experience may be reported as such and must not be relabelled as universally established scientific proof.

## 6. Medical-safety standard

Editorial News is not a substitute for individualized care.

Articles must not:

- diagnose an identifiable reader or patient;
- issue a personal prescription;
- advise delay of emergency or essential in-person care;
- encourage abrupt discontinuation of prescribed treatment without professional assessment;
- promise guaranteed cure or absence of risk;
- present dosage, potency, or treatment instructions as universally safe without the required policy and review.

Emergency content must direct readers to appropriate local emergency or urgent in-person care.

Applicable articles display the approved public-information disclaimer from the contract addendum.

## 7. Patient privacy and consent

No article, image, attachment, caption, source, metadata, revision note, or public interaction may expose direct patient identifiers.

Clinical material must be anonymized and minimized. Consent, when applicable, must be documented through an approved private workflow and does not authorize unnecessary disclosure.

Identifiable clinical photographs require:

- documented informed consent;
- verified authority to publish;
- defined scope and duration of use;
- editorial and privacy approval;
- secure retention of the consent record;
- a withdrawal and correction procedure where legally or ethically applicable.

Until that workflow is implemented and approved, identifiable clinical photographs remain prohibited.

## 8. Headline and summary standard

Headlines and summaries must be:

- accurate;
- proportionate to the evidence;
- clear about uncertainty;
- free from false urgency and clickbait;
- consistent with the visible article and sources;
- free from unsupported universal or miracle claims.

A qualification essential to accuracy must not be hidden only in the article body.

## 9. Quotations and interviews

Quotations must be verified and attributed. Editing for length or clarity must not change meaning.

Interview subjects should be told the nature of the interview and whether remarks are on the record. Anonymous quotation requires heightened editorial approval, verification of identity by an authorized editor, documented reason for anonymity, and protection of the source’s identity in all public projections.

Synthetic, reconstructed, or AI-generated quotations are prohibited.

## 10. Images, documents, and AI-generated media

Every public media item requires:

- source or photographer;
- copyright owner;
- license or permission basis;
- caption;
- descriptive alt text;
- consent status where relevant;
- an AI-generated disclosure where applicable.

AI-generated illustrations must not:

- impersonate a real patient, doctor, researcher, institution, event, document, or scene;
- fabricate evidence;
- imply photographic documentation where none exists;
- contain invented charts, labels, signatures, prescriptions, or test reports presented as real.

Materially altered images must be disclosed when the alteration could affect interpretation.

## 11. Attribution and copyright

Editorial staff must write original text. Facts may be reported, but protected expression must not be copied beyond limited lawful quotation.

Attribution must identify the source of claims, images, data, and quotations. Removing watermarks, credits, or ownership notices to disguise provenance is prohibited.

When a source has a correction, retraction, or update, the Newsroom must assess whether its article requires correction or retraction.

## 12. Conflict of interest

Contributors, editors, reviewers, and institutions must disclose material interests relevant to the article, including:

- clinic or institutional affiliation;
- product ownership;
- consultancy;
- sponsorship;
- paid travel or event support;
- research authorship;
- commercial partnership;
- supplied content;
- family or close professional interest where material.

An editor with a material conflict should be recused from the decisive review when practical. A conflict does not automatically invalidate a source, but nondisclosure is an editorial defect.

## 13. Corrections

Corrections must be prompt, proportionate, and transparent.

### Minor correction

A typographical, grammatical, or formatting correction that does not alter meaning may remain in revision history without a prominent notice.

### Material correction

A correction affecting a name, date, quotation, attribution, result, conclusion, context, image, or other material fact requires a public correction note linked to the corrected revision.

The note must state what was corrected without repeating private or harmful material unnecessarily.

## 14. Retractions

Retraction is required when the central article is no longer reliable or safe because of fabrication, serious factual error, privacy breach, unlawful content, manipulated evidence, invalid source foundation, or another material defect that cannot be corrected adequately.

Retraction must:

- preserve a public accountability notice;
- remove the article from normal promotion and recommendations;
- invalidate caches and distribution feeds;
- retain authorized private evidence for investigation and audit;
- avoid republishing the harmful content in the retraction notice.

## 15. Breaking News

Breaking News must be both urgent and verified sufficiently for publication. The label is temporary and expires automatically.

Breaking coverage must:

- identify what is known and unknown;
- cite the best available source;
- avoid speculation presented as fact;
- update or correct promptly;
- never bypass patient privacy, medical review, or legal/editorial restrictions;
- use accessible presentation without forced or distracting motion.

## 16. Editorial review and separation of duties

Ordinary contributors and submitters cannot publish their own work. Applicable articles require editorial, fact-check, and medical/scientific review before publication.

A reviewer must not approve on behalf of another reviewer. Decisions are tied to authenticated identity, object, revision, time, and review type.

Founder or institutional publishing authority does not remove the requirement to preserve privacy, audit, correction, and source accountability.

## 17. Submission policy

Verified doctors and approved contributors may submit News material, but submission does not guarantee publication.

Submitters must declare:

- ownership or permission for submitted text and media;
- source URLs and supporting documents;
- institution and event details where applicable;
- conflicts of interest;
- sponsorship or payment;
- absence of unauthorized patient identifiers;
- whether AI tools materially assisted content or media creation.

The Newsroom may request information, reject, edit, correct, archive, or retract submitted material under the frozen workflow.

## 18. Translation policy

Translations must preserve meaning, evidence grade, public labels, disclaimers, correction history, and source relationships.

Machine translation may assist drafting but cannot self-publish. Medical terminology requires competent review where material. Published translations must identify the original article and responsible translator/reviewer.

## 19. Comments and community interaction

Comments on News are governed by the existing Phase 3 comment and moderation policy after News object support is explicitly approved.

Comments must not be treated as verified sources merely because they appear below a News article. Identifiers, misinformation, harassment, spam, and unsafe medical advice remain subject to moderation and reporting controls.

## 20. Transparency record

A public News article should display, as applicable:

- article type and disclosure label;
- author or institutional author;
- publication and update dates;
- reviewing editor where policy allows;
- source citations;
- evidence or limitation note;
- conflict disclosure;
- image credit;
- medical disclaimer;
- correction or retraction history.

Private notes, source identities promised confidentiality, contact details, capabilities, and internal deliberations are never part of the public transparency record.

## 21. Editorial-policy enforcement

Potential responses to a breach include:

- request for revision;
- additional review;
- rejection;
- removal from scheduling;
- correction;
- retraction;
- temporary gate closure;
- contributor restriction;
- incident response and rollback.

The severity, intent, public harm, privacy risk, and recurrence determine the response.

## 22. Policy acceptance

This policy is accepted for implementation planning when:

- the contract addendum and architecture reference it;
- editorial UI and automated tests implement its mandatory decisions;
- staging demonstrates that private and public projections remain separated;
- the administrator records approval.

Policy acceptance is not production publication authorization.
